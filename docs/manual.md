# Welcome
Micropolis is a classic city-building simulation, released as open source. You are the mayor: zone land, lay roads and power, set taxes, and keep the people happy while the city grows on its own.

Press Start and choose How to play to open this manual. The city waits while you read.

## Using this manual
- Circle Pad or C-Stick up/down: scroll the text (hold to keep scrolling).
- D-Pad: move between the sections on the touch screen. L / R: previous / next section.
- Tap a section on the touch screen to jump to it.
- B, Start or Close: back to the game.

## The two screens
The touch screen is a flat map you build on with the stylus. The top screen is a 3D close-up that follows your cursor, with the city's money, date, population, demand bars, a minimap and the latest message.

# Stylus controls
| Tap | use the tool on that tile |
| Drag | paint road, rail, power line, park or bulldozer in one stroke |
| Drag a zone | the box follows the stylus; lift it to build (yellow = OK, red = blocked or no cash) |
| Hold still | what is here? (query) |
| L or R + drag | scroll the map |
| Near an edge | dragging scrolls the map |

## Top bar
| $ funds | open the budget |
| Speed | tap to cycle pause, slow, medium, fast |
| Overlay | tap to cycle the map overlays |
| ! | jump to the latest alert |
| Menu | scenarios, load, save, disasters, this manual |

## Tool strip
Bulldozer, Road, Rail, Power, Residential, Commercial, Industrial, and a last slot holding your latest pick from the full grid of 16 tools. Tap that slot again to open the grid.

# Buttons

# Your first city
Start with New city or Dullsville. The clock is paused: nothing happens until you press Select or tap the speed button.

1. Power. Build a Coal plant ($3000) a little away from where the town will be: it pollutes. Nuclear is cleaner but costs $5000.
2. Roads. Draw a short grid of roads from the plant towards open flat land. Roads cost $10 a tile; water crossings become bridges.
3. Zones. Place 3x3 Residential, Commercial and Industrial zones so each touches a road. Start with roughly 3 R : 1 C : 1 I.
4. Connect power. Zones pass power to the zones they touch. Run Power Lines ($5) from the plant to the first zone. A lightning bolt on a zone means it has no power.
5. Run the clock. Press Select. People move in within a few months. Messages appear on the top screen; tap ! to go there.
6. Read the bars. The R C I bars show demand: above the line, build more of that zone; below it, you have too many.
7. Add services as the city grows: police, fire stations and parks. Keep about $5000 in reserve.

# Zones and demand
| Residential | green: where people live; needs jobs nearby |
| Commercial | blue: shops and offices; grows once the population is sizeable |
| Industrial | yellow: factories, jobs and pollution; a seaport boosts it |

## When a zone grows
- it has power
- a road is within 3 tiles
- demand for its type is positive
- land value, crime and pollution are not dreadful (use Query to check)

Density then rises on its own at no further cost.

## Land value
It goes up near water, parks, forest and the city centre, and down near industry, pollution, crime and traffic. High value means richer, taller buildings. The population counter only counts residential zones.

# Power, roads and rail
## Power
A plant powers a connected network of zones and lines: Coal about 20 zones, Nuclear about 60. Brownouts (blinking bolts) mean the plant is overloaded: build another one.

## Roads
$10 a tile. Traffic grows with density; heavy traffic (see the Traffic overlay) lowers land value and stops growth. Add parallel roads or rail.

## Rail
$20 a tile. Carries commuters without traffic. Zones next to rail count it like a road. Roads and rail both decay without funding (Budget: Roads).

# Budget and taxes
Open it with X or by tapping your funds. Taxes are collected every January.

| Tax rate | 7% is comfortable; above 9% people leave |
| Roads | keeps roads and rail from crumbling |
| Police / Fire | at 100% stations work at full strength |
| Cash flow | must stay positive over a year |

Tap a row, then - or +; Done closes it. Running out of money stops building, not the city.

# Map overlays
ZR or the Overlay button. The overlay tints the map and the minimap.

| Power | blue = no power; fix these first |
| Pollution | from industry, traffic, coal and the airport |
| Crime | dense, poor, unpoliced areas |
| Land value | what people pay to live there |
| Traffic | congested roads |
| Density | people per tile |
| Fire / Police | the reach of funded stations |

# Disasters
Menu > Disasters triggers one; some happen at random in long games.

| Fire | spreads along buildings and trees; bulldoze a firebreak |
| Flood | water spreads from the shore, then recedes |
| Tornado | wanders, destroying tiles |
| Earthquake | shakes the whole map; fires follow |
| Monster | drawn by pollution; nothing stops it |
| Meltdown | a nuclear plant explodes; the area stays radioactive |

# The scenarios
Each starts a real city with a crisis and a goal, judged after a time limit.

| Dullsville 1900 | boring town, 10 years: grow to a metropolis |
| San Francisco 1906 | earthquake, 5 years: rebuild to a metropolis |
| Hamburg 1944 | firebombing, 5 years: firebreaks, fund fire fully |
| Bern 1965 | traffic, 10 years: rail and more roads |
| Tokyo 1957 | monster attack, 5 years: rebuild and cut pollution |
| Detroit 1972 | crime, 10 years: police everywhere, parks |
| Boston 2010 | meltdown, 5 years: write off the hot zone |
| Rio de Janeiro 2047 | floods, 10 years: build on high ground |

# Strategy notes
- Industry on one side, homes on the other, commerce in between, with parks or forest as a buffer.
- Grid your roads with blocks 3 tiles deep, so every zone tile is near a road.
- Only zone while the R C I bar is above the line.
- Build rail early in big cities: traffic usually stops growth around 50,000.
- Water edges and hills raise land value: put homes there, industry inland.
- Check the Power overlay after every build.

# Files, saving and 3D
- Menu > Save city writes sdmc:/3ds/micropolis/autosave.cty, which loads automatically next time.
- Menu > Load city lists the autosave first, then every .cty in sdmc:/3ds/micropolis/cities/ (the classic 1989 city format, so classic city files work).
- The 3D slider sets the depth of the top screen: buildings rise with their density, vehicles are nearest, and the cursor sits on whatever it points at.
- A manual.txt in sdmc:/3ds/micropolis/ replaces this manual's text.
