== 1. CONTROLS  (1/2): STYLUS
The touch screen is the map you build on. The
top screen is a 3D close-up that follows you.
Tap            use the tool on that tile
Drag           paint road, rail, power line,
               park or bulldozer in one stroke
Drag a zone    the box follows; lift to build
               (yellow = OK, red = blocked)
Hold still     what is here? (query)
L or R + drag  scroll the map
Tool strip     8 tools; the last slot is your
               pick from the full grid - tap
               it again to open the grid
Top bar        $ budget, speed, overlay,
               ! jump to the latest alert, menu
== 1. CONTROLS  (2/2): BUTTONS
D-pad          move the cursor
Circle pad     scroll the map
A              use the tool (hold + D-pad
               to paint a line)
B              bulldozer; B again = last tool
L / R          previous / next strip tool
Y              zoom the map out / in
X              budget window
Select         speed: pause, slow, med, fast
Start          menu: scenarios, load/save,
               disasters, this manual
ZR             next map overlay
ZL             swap the screens (any time)
Dragging near an edge scrolls the map.
== 2. YOUR FIRST CITY  (1/2)
Start with "New city" or Dullsville. The clock
is PAUSED: nothing happens until you press
Select (or tap the speed button on the top bar).

1. POWER. Build a Coal plant ($3000) a little
   away from where the town will be - it
   pollutes. Nuclear is cleaner but $5000.
2. ROADS. Draw a short grid of roads from the
   plant toward open flat land. Roads cost $10
   a tile; water crossings become bridges.
3. ZONES. Place 3x3 Residential, Commercial and
   Industrial zones so that each touches a
   road. Roughly 3 R : 1 C : 1 I to begin.
== 2. YOUR FIRST CITY  (2/2)
4. CONNECT POWER. Zones conduct power to the
   zones they touch. Run Power Lines ($5) from
   the plant to the first zone; after that,
   adjacent zones pass it on. A lightning bolt
   on a zone means it is unpowered.
5. RUN THE CLOCK. Press Select. People move in
   within a few months. Messages appear at the
   foot of the top screen; tap ! to go there.
6. READ THE BARS. The R C I bars show demand.
   A bar above the line = build more of that
   zone. Below the line = you have too many.
7. ADD SERVICES as the city grows: Police,
   Fire, Parks. Keep $5000 or so in reserve.
== 3. ZONES AND DEMAND  (1/2)
RESIDENTIAL (green)   where people live.
  Grows from empty lots to houses to blocks of
  flats as density rises. Needs jobs nearby.
COMMERCIAL (blue)     shops and offices.
  Grows slowly early on; it needs a sizeable
  population and, for the top tiers, an airport.
INDUSTRIAL (yellow)   factories.
  Provides jobs and exports. Pollutes heavily
  and lowers land value around it. A seaport
  boosts it.

Each zone is 3x3 tiles. Drag it into place and
lift the stylus; red box = blocked or no cash.
== 3. ZONES AND DEMAND  (2/2)
A zone grows when ALL of these hold:
  - it is powered
  - a road is within 3 tiles
  - demand for its type is positive
  - land value / crime / pollution are not
    dreadful (use Query to check)
Density rises on its own, no further payment.

LAND VALUE goes up near water, parks, forest
and the city centre; down near industry,
pollution, crime and traffic. High value =
richer, taller buildings.
The population counter only counts R zones.
== 4. POWER, ROADS, RAIL
POWER: a plant powers a connected network of
zones and lines. Coal ~ 20 zones, Nuclear ~ 60.
Lines cost $5 (more over water) and conduct in
straight runs; zones themselves pass power to
neighbours they touch. Brownouts (blinking
bolts) mean the plant is overloaded: build
another one.
ROADS: $10. Traffic grows with density; heavy
traffic (see the Traffic overlay) lowers land
value and blocks growth. Add parallel roads.
RAIL: $20. Carries commuters without traffic.
Zones next to rail count it like a road.
Both decay without funding (Budget: Roads).
== 5. THE TOOLS  (1/3)
Residential  $100  3x3  homes
Commercial   $100  3x3  shops, offices
Industrial   $100  3x3  factories
Fire Dept    $500  3x3  covers ~15 tiles.
  Fires spread tile to tile; without coverage
  they burn until the fuel runs out. Needs
  Budget funding to be effective.
Police Dept  $500  3x3  lowers crime ~15 tiles.
  Crime rises with density and falls with land
  value and police coverage. Several stations
  for a big city.
Query  free  1x1  inspect any tile: zone type,
  density, land value, crime, pollution, growth.
== 5. THE TOOLS  (2/3)
Power Line  $5   1x1  carries power. Draw by
  holding A while moving.
Bulldozer   $1   1x1  clears trees, rubble,
  buildings, fills shoreline. B is a shortcut.
  Bulldozing a 3x3 zone clears the whole zone.
Rail        $20  1x1  see chapter 4.
Road        $10  1x1  see chapter 4.
Park        $10  1x1  raises land value and
  absorbs pollution. Trees do the same for
  free - don't bulldoze every forest.
Stadium   $5000  4x4  once population passes
  ~150,000 the game asks for one; without it
  residential growth stalls.
== 5. THE TOOLS  (3/3)
Seaport   $3000  4x4  must touch water on one
  side. Boosts industrial demand; the city
  asks for one around 15,000 pop.
Coal Power $3000 4x4  cheap power, heavy
  pollution. Place downwind - far from homes.
Nuclear   $5000  4x4  clean, 3x the output of
  coal. Small yearly meltdown risk, which
  contaminates the area for decades.
Airport  $10000  6x6  unlocks the top
  commercial densities; asked for around
  50,000 pop. Noisy and polluting: keep it
  away from residential zones. Needs a lot of
  flat land.
== 6. BUDGET AND TAXES
Open with X or the Budget button. Taxes are
collected every January.
TAX RATE  7% is the comfortable default. Above
  9% people start leaving; below 5% is a short
  term growth boost you cannot afford long.
ROADS fund    keeps roads and rail from
  crumbling; underfunded roads turn to rubble.
POLICE / FIRE fund   at 100% stations work at
  full strength; at 50% their radius shrinks.
CASH FLOW  must stay positive over a year.
  A deficit auto-cuts services. Running out
  of money freezes building, not the city.
Tip: the earliest years are the tightest.
== 7. MAP OVERLAYS  (ZR)
Shown on the minimap and, lightly, on the city.
Power       red = unpowered zone. Fix first.
Pollution   from industry, traffic, coal, the
            airport. Lowers land value, slows
            residential growth.
Crime       dense, poor, unpoliced areas.
Land value  what people will pay to live there.
Traffic     congested roads; add roads / rail.
Density     population per tile.
Fire cover  radius of funded fire stations.
Police      radius of funded police stations.
Growth      where zones grew (green) or
            shrank (red) recently.
== 8. DISASTERS
Start > Disasters lets you trigger one; a few
also happen at random in long games.
Fire        spreads along buildings and trees.
            Bulldoze a firebreak; fire stations
            put it out.
Flood       water spreads from the shore then
            recedes, leaving rubble.
Tornado     wanders randomly, destroying tiles.
Earthquake  shakes the whole map; fires follow.
Monster     attracted by pollution. Walks and
            smashes; nothing stops it.
Meltdown    a nuclear plant explodes; the area
            is radioactive and unusable for
            years. Rebuild elsewhere.
== 9. THE SCENARIOS  (1/2)
Each starts a real city with a crisis and a
goal, judged after a time limit.
DULLSVILLE 1900   Boring town, 10 years.
  Goal: metropolis (pop ~20,000). Bulldoze and
  rezone around the centre; raise taxes early.
SAN FRANCISCO 1906   Earthquake. 5 years.
  Goal: metropolis. Pause, bulldoze rubble,
  fix power, rebuild roads first.
HAMBURG 1944   Firebombing. 5 years.
  Goal: metropolis. Firebreaks with the
  bulldozer; fund fire at 100%.
BERN 1965   Traffic. 10 years.
  Goal: low traffic. Rail lines, more roads,
  move industry outward.
== 9. THE SCENARIOS  (2/2)
TOKYO 1957   Monster attack. 5 years.
  Goal: pop ~15,000 and low pollution. Let it
  rampage, then rebuild; cut pollution so it
  does not return.
DETROIT 1972   Crime. 10 years.
  Goal: crime down. Police stations everywhere
  dense, fund police 100%, raise land value
  with parks.
BOSTON 2010   Nuclear meltdown. 5 years.
  Goal: pop ~75,000 and low pollution. Write
  off the contaminated zone; build west.
RIO DE JANEIRO 2047   Floods. 10 years.
  Goal: pop ~150,000. Build on high ground,
  away from the shore.
== 10. STRATEGY NOTES
- Industry on one side of the city, homes on
  the other, commercial in between. Parks and
  forest as a buffer.
- Grid your roads with 3-wide blocks between
  them: every tile of a 3x3 zone is then within
  reach of a road, with no wasted land.
- Don't overbuild. Place zones only while the
  R C I bar is above the line; empty zones
  cost money to maintain and never pay tax.
- Rail early in big cities; traffic is the
  usual reason growth stops around 50,000.
- Water edges and hills raise land value: put
  residential there, industry inland.
- Check the Power overlay after every build.
== 11. FILES, SAVING, 3D
SAVE: Start > Save city writes
  sdmc:/3ds/micropolis/autosave.cty, loaded
  automatically the next time you start.
LOAD: Start > Load city lists every .cty in
  sdmc:/3ds/micropolis/cities/ - the original
  classic 1989 city format, so classic files
  from the internet work unchanged.
3D: the slider sets the depth. Water, roads and
  land sit on the screen plane; buildings rise
  with their density; vehicles are nearest and
  the cursor sits on whatever it points at.
  The top screen shows it; the touch map is 2D.
  Load also lists autosave.cty first.
